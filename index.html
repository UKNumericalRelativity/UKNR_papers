Mapping of Spacetimes with Neural Networks
▶ Abstract
Estuti Shukla, David Radice, Rossella Gamba, Sebastiano Bernuzzi
Gauge freedom in numerical relativity complicates the interpretation of simulation coordinates and the comparison of different descriptions of the same spacetime. We present a data-driven neural network method for learning coordinate maps from numerical metric data to a prescribed target gauge. The network is trained by matching the transformed source metric to the target metric, without assuming a functional form for the coordinate transformation. We first validate the method on analytical Schwarzschild and Kerr geometries, recovering known transformations up to the isometries of the target spacetime. We then apply it to numerical black hole evolutions in moving-puncture coordinates. For a non-spinning black hole, the learned map relates the time-dependent numerical metric to a stationary Schwarzschild metric in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates. For a spinning puncture, the method provides an approximate identification with a Kerr background despite departures from Kerr in the numerical spacetime. We use this identification to construct curvature perturbations and evolve them with the Teukolsky equation, obtaining waveforms that closely match those extracted from the full nonlinear simulation. These results demonstrate that learned coordinate maps can relate numerical spacetimes to analytical backgrounds, with potential applications to cross-code validation and black hole perturbation theory.

Numerical-Relativity Particle-in-Cell Simulations: Self-Gravitating Collisionless Matter in Dynamical Spacetimes
▶ Abstract
Jiaxi Wu, Elias R. Most
Gravitational collapse and instability, black-hole dynamics in systems with large scale separations, and questions surrounding extremal black hole formation require full nonlinear studies using accurate and adapted numerical tools. To this end, we present a new algorithm to study collisionless relativistic systems using a fully kinetic description of matter coupled to the evolving spacetime. In detail, we present a numerical-relativity particle-in-cell (NRPIC) framework for solving the Einstein--Vlasov system in three spatial dimensions. Implemented on top of the performance-portable AthenaK code, the method couples particle dynamics to Z4c spacetime evolution in moving puncture gauge through conservative stress-energy deposition on adaptively refined meshes. Our implementation makes use of advances in the computational modeling of relativistic plasmas using modern PIC models, now made available to general-relativistic gravity via an equivalent electrodynamic formulation. We introduce a Boris-type particle pusher that exploits the electrodynamic (Lorentz force) form of the geodesic force. We validate the coupled evolution against spherical dust collapse, follow prolate collapse through collisionless caustics into black-hole formation, and investigate the stability and nonlinear migration of relativistic clusters. Highlighting its potential to study gravitational instability in NR, we simulate the collapse of two overdensities within a common collisionless envelope, following black-hole formation, binary inspiral and merger, and subsequent mass accretion onto the remnant.

Pushing the accuracy of the quasi-circular baseline for the SEOBNR family of models
▶ Abstract
Cheng Foo, Alessandra Buonanno, Héctor Estellés, Lorenzo Pompili
Gravitational-wave observations have opened a new era of astronomy, with accurate modeling of binary black hole signals becoming increasingly important for source characterization and astrophysical inference as detector sensitivities improve. We present SEOBNRv5iHM, an updated quasi-circular, aligned-spin inspiral-merger-ringdown gravitational-waveform model with higher-order modes, constructed within the effective-one-body formalism and building upon SEOBNRv5HM. The improvements mainly concern the late inspiral and the merger-ringdown: we remove the non-quasi-circular corrections used in previous models and instead introduce a phenomenological intermediate region between the inspiral and the ringdown, which allows the ringdown attachment time to be chosen independently for each mode, including at the peak of the mode's amplitude. The model is calibrated and validated against 591 quasi-circular aligned-spin waveforms from the third Simulating eXtreme Spacetimes catalog. For the $(2,2)$ mode, when considering the maximum unfaithfulness against numerical relativity over a total mass range of 10-300$M_\odot$, SEOBNRv5iHM achieves a median of $4.25\times10^{-5}$, a factor of 4.5 below SEOBNRv5HM, and a factor of 3-8 below other semi-analytical models, with $84%$ of cases below $10^{-4}$. For $M\ge150 M_\odot$, the median drops to $9.7\times10^{-6}$, comparable to the numerical-relativity surrogate NRHybSur3dq8. For polarization mismatches at inclination $π/3$, with modes up to $\ell_\mathrm{max} = 5$, the median maximum unfaithfulness is $4.2\times10^{-4}$, a factor of 2.6 below SEOBNRv5HM, 1.9 below IMRPhenomTHM, and 6-11 below the other semi-analytical models. Since spin-precessing and eccentric models are built on top of the quasi-circular, aligned-spin baseline, these improvements will make future SEOBNR models more accurate, reducing waveform systematics in upcoming analyses.

A Transformer model for precessing binary black-hole merger waveforms calibrated to numerical relativity
▶ Abstract
Jameson Dong
We present a Transformer model for merger-centered gravitational waveforms from precessing binary black holes. Given the mass ratio and two three-dimensional spin vectors, the network predicts quintic B-spline coefficients for all 21 signed spin-weighted spherical-harmonic strain modes through $\ell=4$ on $[-1100,80]M$ ($M$ being the total binary mass), enforcing binary-exchange and equatorial-reflection equivariance and yielding $C^4$ waveforms in time with exact automatic differentiation. The model is pretrained on numerical-relativity (NR) waveforms from the SXS and RIT catalogues together with synthetic NRSur7dq4, SEOBNRv5PHM, and perturbative waveforms, and fine-tuned on SXS strain data. On 176 held-out SXS waveforms (165 physical systems), it achieves a mean balanced root-mean-square strain error of $0.023$ and a mean detector mismatch at $120\,M_\odot$ of $0.059%$.

Spacetime Evolution Induced by a Massive Cosmic String in Numerical Relativity
▶ Abstract
Yoshihiro Kishimoto, Cristian Joana, Teruaki Suyama
Cosmic strings are one-dimensional topological defects formed during phase transitions in the early universe. ''Massive'' cosmic strings possess a sufficiently large tension to close their transverse space, forming a conical singularity outside the string, as a static gravitational effect. In a dynamical context, such strings are expected to drive topological inflation. In this work, we investigate the spacetime evolution induced by the formation of a massive cosmic string in a radiation-dominated cosmological background, imposing cylindrical symmetry. Using full numerical relativity simulations, we observe that the string core undergoes topological inflation and that its boundary moves inwards superluminally, which causally disconnects the core from the outer universe. Between the core and the asymptotic regions, a nearly matter-dominated decelerating region originates sourced by the oscillating string fields. This region is bounded by a local maximum (''belly'') and local minimum (''throat'') of the circumferential radius, while the throat recedes outwards at about 10% the speed of light. Furthermore, a gravitational wavefront is emitted from the string, characterizing the light cone of the string formation. Even behind the wavefront, the exterior region no longer exhibits a ''conical'' structure. Based on the simulation results, we construct a Penrose diagram, reflecting the causal structure of the entire spacetime.

Observational Test of the Maximum-Entropy Conjecture for Black-Hole Mergers with GW250114
▶ Abstract
Yang Jiang, Qing-Guo Huang
The remnants of binary black-hole (BBH) mergers are accurately described by numerical relativity, but the physical principle governing the selection of the final states remains unclear. A recently proposed maximum-entropy conjecture suggests that a BBH merger produces the Kerr BH that maximizes the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, subject to the post-Newtonian formalism or balance laws for energy and angular momentum. In this work, we test this conjecture with the event GW250114, inferring the progenitor and remnant properties from the observed pre-merge and post-merge signals, separately. We construct the surrogate Kerr entropy associated with candidate remnant states along the inferred energy-angular-momentum trajectory. We then compare the maximum-entropy state with the independently inferred mass and dimensionless spin of the final BH. We find that the conjecture predicts a remnant mass of $M_f=70.0^{+0.35}_{-0.21}\,M_\odot$ and spin of $χ_f=0.70^{+0.00}_{-0.01}$, compared with the ringdown estimates $M_f={72.3}^{+3.6}_{-3.7}\,M_\odot$ and $χ_f=0.74^{+0.05}_{-0.07}$. The entropy deviation is consistent with zero within the $68%$ credible interval for our fiducial analysis, and variations in the pre-merger truncation time and quasi-normal modes (QNM) content reveal no compelling discrepancy with the conjecture.

Kerr metric perturbations in Lorenz gauge via numerical relativity methods
▶ Abstract
Nami Nishimura, Nils L. Vu, Adrià Gispert-Fernández, Cillian Kelly, Lawrence E. Kidder, Benjamin Leather, Thomas Osburn and 3 others
Gravitational self-force calculations are needed to accurately describe the motion of a small body in a spinning (Kerr) black hole spacetime, which is vital to predict gravitational wave signals from asymmetric-mass compact binaries. Accurate calculations for the upcoming LISA mission require a minimum of second perturbative order in the mass ratio, which has thus far only been achieved for nonspinning (Schwarzschild) black holes due to nonlinear complexities of the Einstein equations. In this work, we calculate Kerr metric perturbations with a new computational approach based on numerical relativity methods, extending recent scalar self-force results to full general relativity. We formulate the perturbative Lorenz-gauge Einstein equations in terms of azimuthal $m$-modes in the frequency domain and solve the resulting elliptic partial differential equations using a spectral discontinuous Galerkin discretization, adaptive mesh refinement, and preconditioned iterative solvers. With our new method, we achieve exponentially convergent first-order gravitational self-force calculations for circular equatorial orbits in Kerr spacetime up to spin $|a|/M=0.998$ (the Thorne limit). Our framework is designed to carry over to second order by delivering accurate and well-behaved first-order data for nonlinear source construction, and as a solver for the second-order metric perturbations themselves.

Binary black hole mergers in massive Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity
▶ Abstract
Harry L. H. Shum, Llibert Aresté Saló, Miguel Bezares, Thomas P. Sotiriou
We study fully non-linear binary black hole mergers in massive Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet (EsGB) gravity with a linear Gauss-Bonnet coupling function. In the absence of a well-developed initial data solver for EsGB gravity, we demonstrate that the conventional workaround solution for massless scalar fields results in confined junk radiation that contaminates late-time dynamics in the massive theory. We propose a solution to this problem by suitably modifying the equations of motion at the early stages of the simulation to tame such junk radiation, thereby allowing us to study the dynamics of the scalar field during the process of a black hole merger. This is followed by a preliminary analysis of the frequency content in the ringdown of the scalar and tensor modes. In particular, the quadrupole scalar mode exhibits a rich ringdown structure: while it is dominated by the spin-2 ``contamination'' frequency in the early stages, its late time morphology becomes primarily determined by the scalar mass frequency.

Disentangling Spin-induced Precession vs. Eccentricity in GW190521 and GW231123
▶ Abstract
Patricia McMillin, Giuseppe Ficarra, Carlos O. Lousto, Richard O'Shaughnessy
We use the more than 2000 full numerical-relativity simulations included in the fifth release of the RIT binary-black-hole waveform catalog to perform Bayesian parameter estimation, via the rapid iterative fitting algorithm (RIFT), for the gravitational-wave events GW190521 and GW231123. We revisit GW190521, which has previously been identified as potentially possessing large eccentricity upon entering the detector band at $20~\mathrm{Hz}$, and confirm this finding: the highest likelihood is obtained from waveforms that are simultaneously eccentric and spin-precessing, yielding $e_{20} =0.207_{-0.147}^{+0.192}$ and $χ_{1,\perp}=0.717_{-0.406}^{+0.194}$. For comparison, both events are also analyzed using exclusively non-precessing (eccentric and quasi-circular) simulations, which yield lower likelihoods in each case. These results indicate that our approach is capable of discriminating between the effects of eccentricity and spin precession in current gravitational-wave signals. The singled out top peak likelihood NR simulations give estimates of the recoil velocities of the merged final holes of 3965km/s for GW190521, and of 2059km/s for GW231123.

Tidal disruption or plunge: the role of neutron star internal structure in black hole-neutron star mergers
▶ Abstract
Yatharth Chaurasiya, Ritam Mallick
The outcome of black hole--neutron star (BHNS) mergers is studied using a general-relativistic mass-shedding condition in the Kerr spacetime, which determines the tidal-disruption radius from the mass ratio, the black hole (BH) spin, the neutron star (NS) compactness, and the ratio of central to average density, $η=\frac{ρ_{c}}{\barρ}$. Comparing this radius with the innermost stable circular orbit, we map the boundary between tidal disruption and plunge for two relativistic mean-field equations of state (EOSs) and for an ensemble of about $10^4$ agnostic EOSs spanning the current uncertainty in the NS radius. We find that the disruption threshold is set mainly by the compactness, but NSs with the same compactness and different $η$ can have different outcomes. Using the tidal disruption radius, we construct a remnant-mass model calibrated against 70 numerical-relativity simulations, which reproduces the remnant mass with an accuracy comparable to existing fits. Applying the disruption criterion and the remnant model to GW200105, GW200115, and the candidate BHNS event GW230529, we find that GW200105 and GW200115 are consistent with a direct plunge. GW230529 lies close to the disruption threshold and may have undergone marginal disruption, leaving only a low-mass remnant.

Considering Numerical Relativity Surrogate Models for AGN Channel Merger Dynamics
▶ Abstract
Shawn Ray, Keefe Mitman, Vera Delfavero, Emily McPike, Jake Postiglione, Harrison E. Cook, Kaila Nathaniel and 4 others
Binary black hole mergers (BBH) are currently observed by the gravitational-wave detectors LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA (LVK). A fraction of these mergers are likely to originate in active galactic nuclei (AGN), but the details of the dynamical processes driving AGN channel mergers remain poorly understood. Monte Carlo For AGN Channel Testing and Simulation (McFACTS) is an open-source, public software used to simulate AGN channel BBH formation and evolution with configurable options for exploring various AGN and Nuclear Star Cluster (NSC) parameter options. Within McFACTS, BBH mergers evolve through gas drag and dynamical interactions, ignoring relativistic effects which can influence merger remnant properties. Here we demonstrate in McFACTS the effect of handing off inspiralling BBH to a numerical relativity (NR) surrogate model, which more accurately predicts the masses, spins, and kick velocities of merger remnants. Compared to a simple analytic approach to calculating merger and remnant properties the NR surrogate model is significantly more accurate at predicting remnant spins, which improve $χ_{eff}$ and $χ_{p}$ calculations, and recoil kicks, parameters especially important for higher generation BBH mergers. Our findings show the NR surrogate significantly changes distributions of remnant spins and kick velocities, as well as showing that alternative methods should be used cautiously in future studies. We also outline some simple models which are computationally efficient and can reproduce much of the NR surrogate model results.

The MARTINI platform II. Tracing r-process nucleosynthesis in neutron star mergers with long-lived remnants
▶ Abstract
Eleonora Loffredo, Diego Vescovi, Albino Perego, Federico Maria Guercilena, Sergio Cristallo
Binary neutron star (BNS) mergers are among the primary sites of the rapid neutron-capture (r-)process. In mergers with long-lived remnants, sustained neutrino irradiation raises the electron fraction of the disc wind ejecta, modifying the resulting nucleosynthesis pattern. Yields from numerical-relativity simulations with accurate neutrino transport are needed to quantify the contribution of BNS mergers to the Galactic production of heavy elements and to interpret kilonova spectra. We present and publicly release tracer trajectories and nucleosynthesis yields for eight BNS mergers forming long-lived neutron star remnants, obtained from general-relativistic simulations with M1 neutrino transport, spanning two equations of state (DD2 and SFHo), and a range of total masses and mass ratios. Passive tracer particles following the dynamical and wind ejecta were post-processed with the nuclear reaction network WinNet up to 1 Gyr. The yields and tracer trajectories are available on the MARTINI platform. At 1 day, the production of light and first-peak elements (He, Ni, Sr, and Y) is similar across models and is primarily enhanced by the neutrino-irradiated disc wind. The yields of heavy nuclei vary by more than one order of magnitude, with unequal-mass binaries producing the largest amounts owing to their more neutron-rich ejecta. Our He yields, particularly in the wind, exceed those of recent studies based on an M0 neutrino scheme, consistent with the stronger neutrino irradiation resulting from an M1 scheme. At 1 Gyr, all models reproduce the second and third solar r-process peaks, but differ in the first peak, the lanthanide region, the mass range between the first and second peaks, and the trans-lead region, reflecting the electron fraction distribution of the ejecta and the dynamical and wind contributions. Two equal-mass models provide a close match to the solar pattern.

Stochastic dynamics of cosmological inflation in general relativity
▶ Abstract
Yoann L. Launay
The theory of Inflation, supplemented by fluctuations of the quantum vacuum, is the leading paradigm to explain the correlations of inhomogeneities at very large distances in the Universe. Particle physics methods fail when strong non-linearity and non-perturbativity are involved, leaving a whole landscape of untestable scenarios of inflation. Starobinsky's stochastic inflation can in principle treat inflationary inhomogeneities nonlinearly and classically as the Hubble radius becomes smaller than their wavelengths, but it was never formulated in full general relativity, which motivates this thesis. Using the Keldysh formalism of nonequilibrium quantum field theory, we first develop a validity criterion for the use of classical computations, and thus simulations, to probe inflationary interactions, finding analytically that the bispectrum can be extracted from slightly sub-Hubble scales in quasi-de Sitter spacetimes. We then provide the first analytical solutions to the constraints for inhomogeneous initial conditions of numerical relativity simulations of inflation, and use them to explore its nonperturbative regime. Our main contribution is to obtain stochastic equations for the full set of inflaton equations in general relativity: this description incorporates the full nonlinear and backreacting evolution, is small-gauge-invariant, and is free of historical assumptions such as the gradient-free separate universe approximation. We implement these equations in the BSSN formulation of numerical relativity, demonstrating their correctness in slow-roll and ultra slow-roll scenarios via the satisfaction of the constraints, with a first look at eternal inflation. Finally, we extend our coarse-graining methods beyond General Relativity and single-field inflation, offering a phenomenological framework with the ambition of serving as a numerical particle collider for the early universe.

Gravitational waves from highly charged binary black holes in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory
▶ Abstract
Ryoto Tomie, Kai Togashi, Kitaro Taniguchi, Ryotaro Kase
We perform fully nonlinear numerical-relativity simulations of electrically charged binary black hole mergers in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton (EMD) theory, varying the magnitude and relative sign of the input charges and the dilaton coupling $a$. In the weak-charge sequence, the dominant gravitational waveforms remain close to the neutral vacuum baseline of general relativity and are visually indistinguishable across the sampled couplings, consistent with earlier EMD simulations. Accurate initial data for Cauchy-characteristic evolution
▶ Abstract
Guido Da Re, Keefe Mitman, Leo C. Stein, Nils Deppe, Himanshu Chaudhary, Dongze Sun, Michael Boyle and 3 others
Accurate gravitational-wave (GW) templates from numerical-relativity (NR) simulations are essential for GW science. These templates are typically computed at finite radii and extrapolated to future null infinity $\mathscr{I}^{+}$, the asymptotic boundary of spacetime. However, this procedure is known to not accurately capture momentum fluxes and memory effects, limiting the accuracy of waveform models and data analyses that rely on them. Cauchy-characteristic evolution (CCE) instead integrates Einstein's equations along outgoing null hypersurfaces from a Cauchy simulation's finite-radius worldtube to $\mathscr{I}^{+}$. Its accuracy, though, has been limited by initial data that produce long-lived spurious transients, which contaminate several cycles of the waveform emitted by the binary coalescence. We identify incorrect initial angular coordinates as the source of these transients. We construct initial data that matches the Cauchy worldtube data through second order in the radial derivatives and solve for the angular coordinates consistent with the resulting radial profile. These improvements remove the unphysical transient: a $10%$ modulation of a binary black hole's $(2,2)$ mode amplitude lasting over $1000\,M$ disappears, and the late-time tail of a head-on black hole collision is recovered. The construction is independent of the Cauchy formulation and allows archived simulations to be reprocessed with CCE, paving the way for improved GW catalogs.

The shape of nonlinearity in binary-black-hole dynamics
▶ Abstract
Tousif Islam
Black-hole perturbation theory remains remarkably accurate beyond its formal small-mass-ratio regime, but what limits this accuracy remains unclear. We develop a modular time-domain Teukolsky framework that isolates finite-mass-ratio corrections to the orbital dynamics while keeping the radiation at first perturbative order. Comparisons with numerical relativity show that the dominant source of discrepancy changes across the waveform. For nonspinning binaries, the available second-order dissipative flux removes $87%$ of the inspiral rate discrepancy, bringing it to within $\sim1%$ of numerical relativity. For precessing binaries, we identify a $ν^2\sin^2ι$ dependence in the inspiral time rescaling that is not explained by the leading orbit-averaged post-Newtonian terms, suggesting finite-mass-ratio corrections to precessional dynamics. Simulating dust collapse: the impact of gauge choices on primordial black hole formation
▶ Abstract

We investigate why numerical relativity simulations of dust collapse in a matter-dominated era frequently fail before primordial black hole formation due to numerical limitations and inappropriate gauge choices. Our approach combines lower-order metric reconstruction in split light-cone gauges with a hyperboloidal, compactified formulation solved on complex radial contours.
