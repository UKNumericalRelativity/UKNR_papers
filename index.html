Stochastic dynamics of cosmological inflation in general relativity
▶ Abstract
Yoann L. Launay
The theory of Inflation, supplemented by fluctuations of the quantum vacuum, is the leading paradigm to explain the correlations of inhomogeneities at very large distances in the Universe. Particle physics methods fail when strong non-linearity and non-perturbativity are involved, leaving a whole landscape of untestable scenarios of inflation. Starobinsky's stochastic inflation can in principle treat inflationary inhomogeneities nonlinearly and classically as the Hubble radius becomes smaller than their wavelengths, but it was never formulated in full general relativity, which motivates this thesis. Using the Keldysh formalism of nonequilibrium quantum field theory, we first develop a validity criterion for the use of classical computations, and thus simulations, to probe inflationary interactions, finding analytically that the bispectrum can be extracted from slightly sub-Hubble scales in quasi-de Sitter spacetimes. We then provide the first analytical solutions to the constraints for inhomogeneous initial conditions of numerical relativity simulations of inflation, and use them to explore its nonperturbative regime. Our main contribution is to obtain stochastic equations for the full set of inflaton equations in general relativity: this description incorporates the full nonlinear and backreacting evolution, is small-gauge-invariant, and is free of historical assumptions such as the gradient-free separate universe approximation. We implement these equations in the BSSN formulation of numerical relativity, demonstrating their correctness in slow-roll and ultra slow-roll scenarios via the satisfaction of the constraints, with a first look at eternal inflation. Finally, we extend our coarse-graining methods beyond General Relativity and single-field inflation, offering a phenomenological framework with the ambition of serving as a numerical particle collider for the early universe.

Gravitational waves from highly charged binary black holes in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory
▶ Abstract
Ryoto Tomie, Kai Togashi, Kitaro Taniguchi, Ryotaro Kase
We perform fully nonlinear numerical-relativity simulations of electrically charged binary black hole mergers in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton (EMD) theory, varying the magnitude and relative sign of the input charges and the dilaton coupling $a$. In the weak-charge sequence, the dominant gravitational waveforms remain close to the neutral vacuum baseline of general relativity and are visually indistinguishable across the sampled couplings, consistent with earlier EMD simulations. For the highly charged binaries with equal-magnitude opposite charges, however, the dominant gravitational strain exhibits visible differences between the EMD cases with $a\neq0$ and the charged Einstein-Maxwell reference at $a=0$. At $a=1$, the coordinate-time interval from the same separation to the waveform-based merger is approximately $62%$ shorter than for the neutral binary. In this high-charge sequence at fixed input charges, increasing $a$ strengthens the scalar dressing while leaving the individual dilaton-weighted horizon charges nearly unchanged. Within the same sequence, increasing $a$ across the sampled values decreases the dipolar electromagnetic-flux peak, increases the scalar-flux peak, and modestly lengthens the merger interval. The sampled like-charged binaries have corresponding merger intervals approximately $15%$-$20%$ longer than the neutral baseline and emit predominantly quadrupolar electromagnetic radiation. At $a=1$ and $a=\sqrt{3}$, the like-charged mergers leave charged remnants with scalar fields consistent with secondary dilaton hair. The opposite-charge remnants instead approach neutral black holes without stationary scalar hair.

Accurate initial data for Cauchy-characteristic evolution
▶ Abstract
Guido Da Re, Keefe Mitman, Leo C. Stein, Nils Deppe, Himanshu Chaudhary, Dongze Sun, Michael Boyle and 3 others
Accurate gravitational-wave (GW) templates from numerical-relativity (NR) simulations are essential for GW science. These templates are typically computed at finite radii and extrapolated to future null infinity $\mathscr{I}^{+}$, the asymptotic boundary of spacetime. However, this procedure is known to not accurately capture momentum fluxes and memory effects, limiting the accuracy of waveform models and data analyses that rely on them. Cauchy-characteristic evolution (CCE) instead integrates Einstein's equations along outgoing null hypersurfaces from a Cauchy simulation's finite-radius worldtube to $\mathscr{I}^{+}$. Its accuracy, though, has been limited by initial data that produce long-lived spurious transients, which contaminate several cycles of the waveform emitted by the binary coalescence. We identify incorrect initial angular coordinates as the source of these transients. We construct initial data that matches the Cauchy worldtube data through second order in the radial derivatives and solve for the angular coordinates consistent with the resulting radial profile. These improvements remove the unphysical transient: a $10%$ modulation of a binary black hole's $(2,2)$ mode amplitude lasting over $1000\,M$ disappears, and the late-time tail of a head-on black hole collision is recovered. The construction is independent of the Cauchy formulation and allows archived simulations to be reprocessed with CCE, paving the way for improved GW catalogs.

The shape of nonlinearity in binary-black-hole dynamics
▶ Abstract
Tousif Islam
Black-hole perturbation theory remains remarkably accurate beyond its formal small-mass-ratio regime, but what limits this accuracy remains unclear. We develop a modular time-domain Teukolsky framework that isolates finite-mass-ratio corrections to the orbital dynamics while keeping the radiation at first perturbative order. Comparisons with numerical relativity show that the dominant source of discrepancy changes across the waveform. For nonspinning binaries, the available second-order dissipative flux removes $87%$ of the inspiral rate discrepancy, bringing it to within $\sim1%$ of numerical relativity. For precessing binaries, we identify a $ν^2\sin^2ι$ dependence in the inspiral time rescaling that is not explained by the leading orbit-averaged post-Newtonian terms, suggesting finite-mass-ratio corrections to precessional dynamics. Near merger, the same dissipative correction removes only $1$--$2%$ of the discrepancy. We show instead that the merger offset is quantitatively accounted for by the difference between the initial Kerr background used in the perturbative evolution and the remnant black hole, with $96%$ of the leading predicted frequency shift arising from the change in spin rather than mass. We release $1571$ time-domain waveforms for $348$ binaries as the Vishveshwara Catalog.

Binary Black Hole Puncture Initial Data with Physics-Informed Neural Networks
▶ Abstract
Thiago Assumpção, Ignacio Brevis, Irene Pitsiladi, Martin Zinzen, Miguel Bezares
We present Pinndorama, a physics-informed neural network (PINN) code for solving the Hamiltonian constraint in the puncture formalism. The networks are trained solely on the governing equation and boundary conditions, without an analytical or perturbative guiding solution, with collocation points sampled in curvilinear coordinates. Training combines two optimization algorithms: an initial stage using adaptive moment estimation (Adam), followed by the self-scaled Broyden quasi-Newton method. We apply this approach to a spherically symmetric test problem with a known exact solution, axisymmetric binary punctures with unequal spins, and a fully three-dimensional GW150914-like configuration with unequal spins and orbital momenta. Comparisons with the NRPyElliptic solver yield relative $L^2$ errors of order $10^{-8}$ in axisymmetry and $10^{-5}$ in three dimensions. In addition, we reduce the hyperbolic-relaxation time in NRPyElliptic by a factor of approximately $4.6$ by training a parametric network that takes the puncture spin as input and provides a physics-informed initial guess to the solver. These results demonstrate that PINNs can provide accurate binary-puncture initial data and complement conventional elliptic solvers in numerical relativity.

How accurate is Spectral Einstein Code (SpEC)? And what limits its accuracy?
▶ Abstract
Himanshu Chaudhary, Mark A. Scheel, Robert Owen, Saul A. Teukolsky, Michael Boyle, Lawrence E. Kidder, Harald P. Pfeiffer
The improved sensitivity of upcoming gravitational-wave detectors will require correspondingly more accurate binary black hole (BBH) simulations. Given the high cost of such simulations, it is essential to understand their sources of error and use computational resources efficiently. We study sources of error in BBH simulations performed with the Spectral Einstein Code (SpEC) and seek to understand what limits their accuracy. In particular, we focus on the inspiral phase because it is the longest-running part of the simulation and accounts for the majority of the waveform error. We find that targeted changes to simulation parameters and resolution in different parts of the computational domain reduce constraint violations and gravitational waveform differences. We also discuss more fundamental limitations on our accuracy that become important at higher resolutions: floating-point errors and large differences in the scales of the fundamental variables. Addressing these limitations will require substantial changes to both the SpEC code and the formulation it uses to evolve Einstein's equations. Many of the improvements and changes discussed in this paper are likely to be relevant to other numerical relativity codes.

Simulating dust collapse: the impact of gauge choices on primordial black hole formation
▶ Abstract
Robyn L. Munoz, Christian T. Byrnes, Marco Bruni, Ian Hawke
We investigate why numerical relativity simulations of dust collapse in a matter-dominated era frequently fail before primordial black hole formation due to numerical limitations and inappropriate gauge choices. Using 3-dimensional fully non-linear simulations of a dust fluid, we detail the advantages of advanced numerical methods and the impact of the lapse and shift evolution equations. The challenge is twofold: 1) conventional lapse slicing evolution equations, highly effective in typical black hole simulations, must be adapted in a cosmological context. We demonstrate how different proposed lapse evolution equations perform, enabling fair comparison, and propose our own. 2) The shift evolution equation's role is to spread out the numerical grid observers for a stable simulation. However, the Gamma-driver evolution fails when confronted with shell-focussing of the dust fluid. We show how the shift damping choice depends on the resolution of the shell-focusing region, suggesting that future work should have spatially varying damping. Our improved gauge choices allow simulations to evolve significantly further and potentially reach black hole formation. The best-performing simulations reach close proximity to a black hole; however, further work on the gauge choice is required for this to be stable.

Numerical simulations of low-mass black hole-neutron star mergers with moderate eccentricities
▶ Abstract
Ivan Markin, Gonzalo Morrás, Tim Dietrich
A confident detection of orbital eccentricity in gravitational-wave signals from black hole-neutron star systems would be a strong indicator of their dynamical formation origin. While eccentric waveform models have recently been developed, their validity for analyzing black hole--neutron star signals has yet to be tested against numerical-relativity simulations. Building upon our previous study of quasicircular black hole--neutron star mergers, we perform simulations of a subset of these systems with orbital eccentricities around five times larger than those inferred for GW200105_162426. Additionally, we perform simulations with magnetized matter to assess whether trapped $g$-mode oscillations persist in strongly magnetized disks. Regarding the emitted gravitational-wave signal, we find that the effective one-body waveform model SEOBNRv5EHM shows reasonable agreement unless tidal contributions become important, and the inspiral-only post-Newtonian models pyEFPE/pyEFPEHM show larger disagreement in the late inspiral. For the equal-mass system, which has the largest effective tidal deformability, eccentric tidal corrections have a small but noticeable impact on the phase, $\sim0.1$ rad. We find an almost negligible impact of eccentricity on the properties of the remnant black hole, and differences of up to a factor of two in the remnant disk mass. Trapped $g$ modes in the accretion disks have the same frequency regardless of the eccentricity, confirming that they depend mainly on the properties of the remnant black hole. We find that when accretion disks are strongly magnetized, they become less dense and have less pronounced structure, and that the trapped $g$ modes previously identified in the unmagnetized cases are efficiently damped.

Cubic Quasinormal Modes in the Black-Hole Ringdown
▶ Abstract
Neev Khera, Huan Yang
We formulate and numerically solve the nonlinear Teukolsky equations through third order, obtaining a first-principles calculation of cubic quasinormal-mode amplitudes for Kerr black holes. Our approach combines lower-order metric reconstruction in split light-cone gauges with a hyperboloidal, compactified formulation solved on complex radial contours. Spin-dependent complex coupling coefficients fix the cubic amplitudes from products of their linear parent amplitudes. We apply these predictions to a nonprecessing numerical-relativity waveform, including cubic products of the $(2,2)$ fundamental mode, its first overtone, and their conjugates. The predicted cubic terms improve agreement over selected early-ringdown fitting windows and shift inferred linear-overtone amplitudes without adding fitting parameters. $3M$ after the peak of $|h_{22}|$, the summed cubic contribution reaches approximately $38%$ of the total $h_{22}$ signal; $M$ is the initial total mass. These results identify an appreciable cubic response in early ringdown.

Scalar wave dark matter imprints on binary black hole mergers: dependence on field mass and density
▶ Abstract
Yu-Peng Zhang, Shao-Wen Wei, Yu-Xiao Liu
Dark matter environments can modify binary black hole mergers and imprint their gravitational-wave signals. We perform numerical-relativity simulations of nonspinning binary black holes immersed in complex scalar-field clouds, considering mass ratios $q=1,2,3,$ and $4$ and varying both the dimensionless scalar-field mass $\hat m_Φ=m_ΦM_0$ and initial amplitude $Π_0$, which controls the density. Our initial data keep the scalar energy-density profile and binary geometry unchanged when varying $\hat m_Φ$ at fixed $Π_0$, allowing the dynamical effect of the field mass to be isolated. We find that the scalar environment generally accelerates the merger and significantly modifies the gravitational-wave signal. For unequal-mass binaries, differential scalar-field accretion changes the horizon-mass ratio during inspiral, weakly at low density but substantially at high density, thereby increasing the binary asymmetry before merger. Dense scalar environments suppress the energy and angular momentum carried by gravitational waves. Lighter scalar fields propagate more efficiently to large radii, whereas heavier fields remain more localized and show stronger inward energy transport. Scalar angular-momentum transport is more complex, showing nonmonotonic behavior and even direction reversal near the binary. For unequal-mass systems, gravitational and scalar radiation both carry net linear momentum, and their relative orientation determines whether the recoil is enhanced or suppressed. The largest kick is $2063.6~{\rm km,s^{-1}}$ for $q=2$, $\hat m_Φ=0$, and $Π_0=0.0100$, compared with $137.8~{\rm km,s^{-1}}$ in vacuum. These results show that the scalar-field mass and density jointly control the binary asymmetry, dynamics, radiative channels, and momentum balance.

Relativistic dynamical friction in Kerr-Schild coordinates
▶ Abstract
Zipeng Wang, Francesca Chadha-Day, Katy Clough, James Maxwell
Simulations of the evolution of matter on static metric backgrounds allow us to estimate backreaction effects like dynamical friction at first order in the matter density. Previous simulations of dynamical friction have used boosted isotropic and quasi-isotropic Kerr coordinates, which simplifies the extraction of the relevant diagnostics but results in a more complicated implementation of the evolution. In particular, the lack of horizon penetrating coordinates means that high resolutions are needed to ensure convergence, and the boosted, spinning black hole metric cannot easily be written in a closed form, making it more costly to evaluate. In this paper we describe how to reformulate the calculation of the diagnostics in the boosted Kerr-Schild frame for the non-spinning case. We verify that our implementation reproduces previous results quantifying dynamical friction in wave-like dark matter in the appropriate regimes. We discuss the subtleties of transforming diagnostic observables between frames in a 3+1 evolution and develop a general framework for this process.

The Gregory-Laflamme Instability of five-dimensional Black Strings in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet Gravity
▶ Abstract
Pau Figueras, Áron D. Kovács, Shunhui Yao
We complement and extend our recent study of the non-linear evolution of the Gregory-Laflamme instability of black strings in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity in five spacetime dimensions. First, we study time-dependent perturbations of uniform black strings in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet theory and determine the growth rates as a function of the coupling. We find that for positive Gauss-Bonnet couplings, the black strings are more unstable than in general relativity in the sense that the growth rates are larger and shorter wavelengths are unstable. In the second part of the paper we show that the hyperbolicity of the equation of motion of Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet theory is determined by an effective metric when the dynamics is restricted to spherical symmetry in the asymptotically flat directions. When the effective field theory becomes strongly coupled, as it happens near the endpoint of the Gregory-Laflamme instability of the black string, this effective metric becomes degenerate and the Cauchy evolution breaks down. Applying this criterion to uniform black strings, we find that for any positive Gauss-Bonnet coupling there is an open set of regular solutions (including the one of minimal thickness) on which the effective metric degenerates in a neighbourhood of the horizon. Therefore, we argue that these solutions cannot be attained from weakly coupled initial data by Cauchy evolution and the solutions of the linearized equations around them cannot be interpreted as approximations to nearby non-linear evolutions. We provide further evidence for this picture with non-linear simulations of the Gregory-Laflamme instability. The non-linear simulations terminate through a loss of hyperbolicity of the Tricomi or Keldysh type, depending on the sign of the Gauss-Bonnet coupling.

Binary Ellis-Bronnikov wormholes in 3+1 numerical relativity: mergers, fly-bys and gravitational waves
▶ Abstract
Nikita M. Shirokov, Ilya Nachevsky
We present what are, to our knowledge, the first 3D numerical-relativity evolutions of wormhole binaries: pairs of massive Ellis-Bronnikov drainholes held open by a ghost scalar field. Each throat is an unstable fixed point whose e-fold time matches the González-Guzmán-Sarbach linear rate to within 1.9%. Two throats obey a charge-sign rule: like scalar charges repel and opposite charges attract, with a measured attraction-to-repulsion ratio of $1.462\pm0.022$ against the predicted 3/2, so only opposite-signed pairs merge. A head-on collision forms a common MOTS around both still-open throats, born with about their combined area. The horizon then loses 28% of its area as it swallows the phantom field, and by $t=100$ lies within 1.4% of the Schwarzschild radius of the pair's mass, still shrinking. Fly-by pairs form no horizon and both mouths inflate, the one nearest capture radiating the most gravitational-wave energy measured. A lone collapsing throat sheds its seeded quadrupole as a burst ringing at the Schwarzschild frequency of the black hole it becomes, and every encounter radiates its own burst of gravitational waves and a scalar dipole of comparable energy and negative sign. A throat survives at most a few orbital periods for any plausible perturbation, and the negative-energy dipole would pump rather than drain the orbit, so these binaries have no inspiral.

Accounting for detector calibration uncertainty in black-hole ringdown analysis
▶ Abstract
Rowina S. Nathan, Shingo Hido, Neil Lu, Mallika R. Sinha, Sizheng Ma, Ling Sun
Detector calibration systematics can bias measurements of a remnant black hole's mass and spin, producing spurious evidence against general relativity if unaccounted for. The ringdown phase in the gravitational-wave signal produced by a binary black-hole merger encodes the remnant properties: at sufficiently late times, the signal is well described by a superposition of quasinormal modes, each with a frequency and damping time set only by the remnant mass and spin. Here we present two complementary approaches for analyzing the ringdown portion of the data to estimate the remnant mass and spin in the presence of a nonnegligible detector calibration error, using the quasinormal-mode rational filter (QNMRF). With a well-measured error, we apply the best-estimated calibration correction to the strain before conducting the standard analysis; with an unknown error, we instead jointly sample the remnant mass, spin and the frequency-dependent calibration error, marginalizing over an uncertainty prior. By injecting the numerical-relativity waveform SXS:BBH:0305 into colored detector noise, at a full-signal matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio of $\sim60$ and with simulated calibration errors of $\pm25%$ in magnitude and $\pm25^i̧rc$ in phase, marginalizing over the calibration uncertainty recovers the true mass and spin within the 90% credible interval, whereas the estimated values are biased if the calibration errors are ignored. We apply the method to GW250207, an event recorded while the Hanford detector response was not well characterized, and recover a remnant consistent with the inspiral-merger-ringdown prediction using the ringdown portion alone, both by correcting for a best-estimated calibration error and by marginalizing over a broad uncertainty prior.

Collisions of Multiple Black Holes: A Numerical Relativity Study
▶ Abstract
Chen-Kai Qiao, Shu-Yuan Liu
Numerical relativity has a profound impact on gravitational theory and gravitational wave astronomy. In this work, we present a systematic study of the head-on collisions of multipule black hole systems within the framework of numerical relativity using the Baumgarte-Shapiro-Shibata-Nakamura (BSSN) formulation. The numerical simulations of the dynamical evolution of gravitational fields are computed using the AMSS-NCKU code. We report numerical results for black hole collisions in several symmetric and non-symmetric configurations, in which the black holes are initially at rest and are described by Bowen-York type initial data. Gravitational radiations from $m \ne 0$ modes become non-negligible only when non-symmetric configurations are encountered. Keywords: Numerical Relativity, Black Holes, Gravitational Waves, N-Body Systems

▶ Abstract

Locating critical solutions in numerical relativity using automatic differentiation
▶ Abstract
David Bambague, Miguel Bezares, Katy Clough, Steven Tobias
Numerical relativity is a term used to describe the solution of the full non-linear Einstein Equations as an initial value problem using numerical methods. Individual simulations are computationally expensive and in many applications the high complexity of the parameter space and non-linear sensitivity of the solutions makes locating particular outcomes of interest challenging. In this work, we demonstrate how auto-differentiation can efficiently find dynamical solutions of interest, using the classic problem of scalar critical collapse as a proof of principle.
